# Reference — Provenance Research & Watermark Analysis

> Distilled from Heawood (1932) *Watermarks Mainly of the 17th and 18th Centuries*; IMCoS (2020) *Guidelines on Map Authentication and Valuation*. Operationalised by the `watermark_lookup` tool and the `ProvenanceMaterialsAdvisor`.

## Core principle
A watermark (wire design sewn onto the paper mould) dates the **paper**, not the map image — a sheet can be printed years after manufacture, and plates can be reprinted on later paper. A watermark is therefore a **terminus post quem for the paper** and a provenance indicator (mill/region), usable as one dating axis among several.

## Provenance chain checklist
1. **Watermark**: hold to light; record motif + countermark initials exactly; query the tool.
2. **Ownership marks**: bookplates, "ex libris", stamps, signatures — each link dated.
3. **Annotations/marginalia**: dated handwriting styles can bracket ownership periods.
4. **Sale/auction catalogue records**: trace prior sales; cite catalogue + lot where possible.
5. **Institutional accession records**: confirm the chain when available.
6. **Reconcile**: each provenance link is a dating/attribution data point, **not** proof of authenticity.

## Guardrail
Watermarks can be copied/re-used; paper can outlive printing. Laboratory/specialist examination is required for definitive paper dating. Refer to an accredited map appraiser for authentication/valuation.
