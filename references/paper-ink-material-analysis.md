# Reference — Paper & Ink Material-Analysis Principles

> Distilled from Heawood (1932), IMCoS (2020), and general paper-conservation knowledge underpinning the skill. Explains *principles* only — definitive material authentication requires laboratory/specialist examination.

## Core principle
Material analysis (paper fibre, ink composition, chain-line spacing, condition) provides supporting dating and authenticity indicators. None of them alone authenticates a map; they are **corroborative axes** that a specialist interprets together.

## Paper dating indicators
- **Fibre type**: rag (linen/cotton) paper pre-c.1850; **wood-pulp** paper post-c.1850 — a fast material terminus post quem.
- **Laid vs wove**: laid paper (visible chain lines) dominant pre-c.1800; wove paper (smooth) common from c.1757 (Whatman) onward.
- **Chain-line spacing**: measured mould signatures can match a specific mill/batch (specialist work).
- **Watermark**: see `provenance-watermarks.md` — dates the paper, not the image.
- **Sheet size/formats**: foolscap, pot, crown, elephant sizes have period/mill associations.

## Ink & colour indicators
- **Iron-gall ink**: common 15th–19th c.; oxidises brown, can bleed through (show-through).
- **Printed vs hand colour**: early maps often sold plain and hand-coloured later — colour may postdate the print by decades.
- **Pigment chronology**: some pigments (e.g. Prussian blue, c.1704; synthetic ultramarine, 1828) are terminus post quem for colouring.
- **Gouache/wash application style**: period conventions differ; stylistic, not definitive.

## Condition indicators (supporting only)
- Foxing, acid burn, brittling: consistent with age but environment-dependent; **not** a reliable dating axis.
- Folds/centrefolds: atlas vs folding-map vs separate-issue format clues.

## Operational checklist
1. Identify paper fibre (rag vs wood-pulp) → pre/post c.1850 bracket.
2. Identify laid vs wove → pre-c.1800 vs c.1757+.
3. Note any watermark + countermark → query watermark tool.
4. Assess ink oxidation/bleed and colour type (printed vs hand; pigment where identifiable).
5. Flag every material claim as **corroborative**, not authenticating.

## Guardrail
Material analysis for authenticity **requires laboratory or specialist examination**. This skill explains principles only. Refer to an accredited map appraiser or paper conservator for definitive assessment.
