# Reference — Print-Technique Dating Methodology

> Distilled from Woodward (1975) *Five Centuries of Map Printing*; Verner (1965) *Copperplate Printing*; Woodward (1996) *Maps as Prints in the Italian Renaissance*. Operationalised by the `print_technique_lookup` tool and the `PrintTechniqueDatingAdvisor`.

## Core principle
A map's printing technique is a **terminus post quem**: the map cannot predate the technique's introduction, and a technique's dominance window brackets the most likely date. Technique is *necessary, not sufficient* — it dates the medium, not the image (a plate can be re-printed decades later).

## Technique chronology (quick table)

| Technique | Dominant window | Key diagnostic | Dating implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woodcut (relief) | c.1450–c.1600 | Raised lines, wood-grain, **no platemark** | Pre-c.1600; woodcut Ptolemy-style → 1470s–1560s |
| Copper engraving (intaglio, burin) | c.1500–c.1820 | **Platemark**, swelling burin lines, plate wear in late impressions | c.1550–c.1820; crisp = early state, worn = late state/re-issue |
| Etching (acid-bitten intaglio) | c.1600 onward | Free flowing lines, often mixed with engraving | c.1600 onward; weak axis alone |
| Steel engraving / steel-faced | c.1822 onward | Extremely fine uniform lines, **no wear** across large editions | Post-c.1822 |
| Lithography (planographic) | c.1796 invented; common c.1820+ | **No platemark**, crayon/wash tones, "drawn" look | Practically post-c.1820; chromolithography mid–late 19th c. |

## Operational checklist (apply in order)
1. **Platemark?** Present → intaglio (copper/steel/etching). Absent → woodcut or lithography.
2. **Line quality.** Knife-cut wood-grain → woodcut. Burin swelling lines → copper. Acid-free-flowing → etching. Ultra-fine uniform → steel.
3. **Tone rendering.** Cross-hatch → intaglio/woodcut. Wash/stipple/crayon → lithography.
4. **Plate wear.** Compare impressions of the same plate; wear sequences states/editions (Koeman methodology).
5. **Cross-check date.** A lithograph cannot predate c.1796; a steel engraving cannot predate c.1822; a woodcut almost certainly predates c.1600.

## Guardrail
Print-technique evidence **cannot authenticate** a map. Re-cut plates and later reprints share technique with originals. Refer to an accredited map appraiser for authentication/valuation.
